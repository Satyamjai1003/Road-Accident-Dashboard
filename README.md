📌 Overview
This project analyzes road accident data (2017–2020) from Addis Ababa, Ethiopia, to uncover trends in casualties, severity, and contributing factors (e.g., road conditions, driver experience, lighting). The Excel Dashboard visualizes insights via interactive charts, pivot tables, and slicers, while the Project Report documents methodologies, findings, and recommendations for road safety improvements.

🎯 Key Objectives
The project addresses 6 critical objectives to identify accident patterns and propose actionable solutions:

Casualty Class vs. Number of Casualties

Identify high-risk groups (e.g., drivers, pedestrians).

Finding: Drivers accounted for 50% of casualties (highlighting need for seatbelt/speeding enforcement).

Light Condition vs. Number of Casualties

Analyze accident rates under daylight/darkness.

Finding: 30% of casualties occurred in dark, unlit areas (urgent need for street lighting).

Gender-wise Distribution of Casualties

Compare male vs. female involvement.

Finding: Males had 3x higher casualties (e.g., 1,609 "High" severity cases vs. 432 for females).

Driving Experience vs. Number of Casualties

Evaluate risk by driver experience level.

Finding: Novice drivers (0–2 years) caused 60% of casualties during overtaking/lane changes.

Road Surface Type vs. Average Casualties

Assess risk by surface (e.g., wet asphalt, gravel).

Finding: Wet asphalt had 35% of casualties despite being 20% of roads (needs skid-resistant coatings).

Accident Severity Analysis

Categorize casualties by severity (Slight/Serious/Fatal).

Finding: 10,415 slight injuries vs. 158 fatalities (prioritize emergency response infrastructure).
